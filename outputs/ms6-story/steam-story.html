CS1710 · Steam Games Dataset · a data story for solo developers

Reach on Steam is earned, not priced

You are about to ship your first commercial game. Should you price it at $4.99 to get players in the door? 99,754 Steam titles say that is the wrong question.

Steam Games Dataset (Kaggle, Steam API + SteamSpy) · 125,855 games · "reach" = estimated-owner band of at least 100,000

1 · Hook

Three out of four games on Steam never pass 20,000 owners.

75%

Developers see the hits, and the games next to theirs. They never see the long tail, because nobody writes about it. Of the games that have any owner estimate at all, only 8.7% ever reach 100,000 owners, and another 26,101 titles are so small that SteamSpy records no estimate.

2 · Rising insight

Cheap does not buy reach. The $20–40 shelf succeeds most often.

Half of all games that reached 100,000 owners cost under $5, so it is tempting to conclude that low prices win. But that shelf also holds 61,000 games. Per game, it is the least likely to reach 100,000 owners, and the $20–40 shelf is the most likely.

3 · Rising insight

Reach rises with how long people play and how many of them can read the game.

Two things that developers can actually build are far more strongly associated with reach than price is. Games that hold players for 6 to 12 hours reach 100,000 owners more than five times as often as games played for under an hour. Games localized into 11 to 20 languages reach it five times as often as single-language games.

Depth and localization each move the odds by 5x. Review score moves them by 1.4x. Price moves them the wrong way.

Associations, not causes. Playtime is only recorded for games with enough players, so the playtime chart describes 25,345 games. The 21+ language dip is mostly very small titles with machine-translated store pages.

4 · Main message

The traits stack. A deep, localized, well-reviewed game reaches 100,000 owners seven times out of ten.

Neither trait works alone. Localizing a game that people drop within an hour does nothing. Depth without localization gets you to one in three. Together they get you past one in two, and adding a review share above 80% takes you to 72%.

5 · Solution

What a solo developer should do with this

  • 01
    Budget for the long tail.Three in four games stay under 20,000 owners. Treat 100,000 as the upside case, not the plan.
  • 02
    Stop pricing for reach.Price to scope. The $10–40 shelves outperform the $0–5 shelf per game, and a higher price funds the next two items.
  • 03
    Build for the sixth hour before the first DLC.Playtime is the single strongest trait we found. Cut features that do not add play, and add ones that do.
  • 04
    Localize to 6–20 languages at launch.Not one, not forty. The 6–20 range is where the reach rate climbs steeply; beyond 20 the association disappears.
  • 05
    Ship Mac and Linux builds if your engine makes it cheap.Three-platform games reach 100k at 16% versus 7% for Windows-only. A modest, consistent edge.
