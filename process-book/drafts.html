CS1710 · Prototype V1 · drafts for views not yet implemented

Three drafts

Digital drafts of the views planned for V2. Numbers are computed from the real data (owner band ≥ 100k) by the pipeline, so the mock-ups show real values.

Draft A · novel view

Peer ladder: describe your game, watch the base rate move

The developer adds one trait at a time. Each step shows the reach rate among games matching every trait so far, how many games that is, and how sure we can be. The ladder makes the main message visible: the right peer group changes the number, and a small peer group widens the uncertainty.

Genre: RPGPrice: $10–20Languages: 5+Platforms: Windows onlyEarly Access+ add trait

Closest peers at the last step with ≥ 100k owners: Bellwright, Nightingale, GRAV, Travellers Rest, Going Medieval, Quasimorph.

Bar = share of matching games with ≥ 100k owners. Whisker = 95% Wilson interval. Dashed line = all games. The badge appears when fewer than 200 games match, which is the point where the comparison should be treated as a hint, not a rate. Traits are only those a developer chooses before launch; playtime and reviews are outcomes and stay out of the ladder.

Draft B

Launch-year cohorts: how long reach takes to accumulate

Question 13 and 14 from MS4, reframed for what a snapshot can answer: games released earlier have had more time to accumulate owners, so recent cohorts look worse than they are. The view puts that on screen before a developer compares against old hits.

Bar = share of games released that year with ≥ 100k owners today; number above = games released that year. Two reading aids are planned: a selector for the developer's genre, and a shaded "too recent to judge" zone for the last two years. The dataset is a single snapshot, so this is reach-by-age, not a trend in player taste.

Draft C

Localization ladder: languages inside the right peer group

Question 8 from MS4 (sketch LL1), moved from peak players to reach and placed inside the developer's own genre and price band, with the store-wide group alongside as the baseline.

Grey = all games, orange = RPG priced $10–20. The step from 2–4 to 5–9 languages is the largest in both groups, which is the decision a solo developer can act on. The genre and price band come from the same shared selection as the comparison tool.
